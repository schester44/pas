## Openkoda (primary competitor)

What they sell (Nov 2026, from their site and GitHub):

- An "AI-configured" PAS for MGAs/coverholders, carriers, brokers, TPAs and insurtechs: AI product builder (plain-English changes, human-approved diff), rating engine, drag-and-drop workflow engine, forms & portals, policy admin, underwriting queue with copilot, claims (FNOL → payout), billing, documents, role dashboards with natural-language reporting, bordereaux, team chat/tasks on records, ACORD-aligned data model, effective-dated product versions with in-flight cases finishing on their starting version, MCP server, managed cloud or on-prem.
- **Pricing**: Business $2,000/mo (single org; _no_ portals, SSO, custom domain, submission APIs, payments, email integrations or MCP), Enterprise $4,000/mo (adds those), On-prem custom. AI is a separate credit subscription ($500–$2,000/mo). Services beyond 5 days are time and materials.
- **No longer open source.** The `openkoda/openkoda` GitHub repo now states the source is no longer published and points to Superkoda, a _generic_ AI-customizable business-app platform. The insurance product is an app on a horizontal low-code platform (historically Java/Spring Boot/Hibernate/Postgres with an MIT core).

Where they're thin or vague (public material only):

- Rating authoring method, rule syntax, form-builder depth, extension languages and endorsement/cancellation math (pro-rata vs short-rate, out-of-sequence) aren't described anywhere public.
- No visible surplus-lines filing, notice compliance, obligation tracking, capacity/delegated-authority enforcement, producer licensing or jurisdiction content. Generic low-code platforms rarely go deep on these, and this is where insurance operations actually hurt.
- Scalability is asserted, not quantified.

How we beat them:

## What stands out: things missing from the first draft of the plan

1. **Config is both UI and code.** Socotra is code-first, Duck Creek is low-code. Do both: config lives in the DB and is edited in the UI, but every product version exports and imports as a deterministic, git-friendly bundle (JSON/YAML + CSV rate tables + templates), with a `pas` CLI for `pull / diff / validate / test / push`. Teams that want PR review get it; teams that don't never see it.
2. **Unsafe-change detection and data evolution.** When a RiskSchema changes, in-force policies were written against the old schema. Classify changes (additive = safe; rename/retype/remove = unsafe and needs a declared migration applied lazily at the next transaction or renewal). Socotra blocks unsafe deploys; we should guide the user through them.
3. **A typed extension (plugin) model for the last 5%.** Config won't cover everything. Offer named extension points (the Socotra list is a good catalog: precommit, validation, rating, underwriting, document selection, document data, installments, cancellation charges, automation) written in **TypeScript**, run in a **sandbox** (V8 isolates / WASM) with time and memory limits, versioned and pinned in the ProductRelease like everything else.
4. **Sandboxes with time travel.** Lifecycle testing (renewals, NOC deadlines, reinstatement windows) is miserable without a controllable clock. Sandbox tenants get a per-tenant `Clock` offset plus "advance to date" that fires due timers. It's cheap because time is already a port.
5. **A generic exposure model.** Coverages attach to _things_: locations, buildings, vehicles, drivers, classes of business. The data dictionary needs **exposure nodes** that coverages, limits, deductibles and premium attach to. Without it, commercial and auto lines can't be modeled and premium can't be broken down per exposure for bordereaux and stat reporting.
6. **Capacity and delegated authority.** For MGAs, the binding authority agreement is the contract that matters: which carrier(s), lines, territories, max limits/TIV, premium caps (aggregate and per-risk), referral-to-carrier conditions, effective/expiry. It must be **enforced at bind** and tracked (aggregate written premium vs cap). Products can be backed by multiple carriers (panel, quota share, fronting), with allocation rules and mid-year capacity swaps.
7. **Bordereaux and regulatory reporting.** Premium (and later claims) bordereaux to capacity providers on schedule, in the provider's format (Lloyd's Coverholder Reporting Standards style), plus statistical reporting (ISO/AAIS stat plans) and state data calls. Configurable report definitions, not code.
8. **A compliance obligation engine.** Surplus-lines filings, diligent-search affidavits, notices, bordereaux, stat reports and FCRA adverse-action notices are all the same shape: _an event creates an obligation with a due date, a responsible party, a channel (adapter) and proof of completion._ One engine for all of them (see PLAN §17).
9. **Producer compliance at bind.** Check the producer is licensed in the risk state for the line (and the surplus-lines licensee for E&S) and appointed with the carrier. Check sanctions (OFAC) on insureds and parties at quote, bind, endorsement and payee changes.
10. **ACORD in and out.** Prefill submissions from ACORD 125/126/140 (PDF or XML) and generate ACORD evidence forms (27/28 property, 25 certificates of insurance for commercial). Downloads to agency management systems.
11. **Accounts and multi-policy.** Accounts (an insured household or business) above policies, for multi-policy discounts, account-level billing and a single customer view. Socotra has accounts.
12. **Book migration and takeover tooling.** The biggest blocker to adoption is moving an in-force book off another system: bulk in-force import (as-of snapshots, not full history), mid-term conversion, renewal-time conversion with a re-rate comparison report.
13. **Data out.** Tenants want their data: CDC/outbox-fed event stream, a warehouse export (Parquet to S3 / Snowflake / BigQuery), and an OpenAPI that covers reads. In an open-source product this is part of the trust story.
14. **AI-native configuration.** Duck Creek is bolting an agentic configurator onto XML. Our config is typed JSON with schemas, static reference checks and test suites, which is close to ideal input for an LLM. Ship an MCP server and in-admin assistant for: drafting a product from a filing or ACORD app, generating rate tables from a filed rate manual, writing decision tables from UW guidelines, explaining a referral or a premium change ("why did this go up 12%?") from the traces. The deterministic engines stay the source of truth; AI only proposes config that goes through the same publish gates.
15. **E-delivery consent and mailing proof.** Electronic delivery of policy documents and notices generally requires recorded consent. Notices sent by mail need proof of mailing. Both are data the system must hold (see PLAN §17.2).
16. **Fees and premium mechanics.** Minimum earned premium, fully earned policy fees, installment fees, inspection fees, short-rate tables, rounding at line-item vs total level, whether fees are taxable per state. These belong in config.
17. **Premium finance and mortgagee billing.** Many personal and E&S policies are financed (premium finance agreements, which create their own cancellation requests) or paid by the mortgagee through escrow. Both drive lifecycle events (finance-company cancellation requests, mortgagee change endorsements, lender evidence requests).

## Positioning: nimble, fast, open

- **Time-to-first-product in days, not quarters.** Starter content packs + UI + tests + sandboxes. Self-serve; no SI required.
- **Developer experience incumbents can't match.** `docker compose up` locally, TypeScript extensions, config-as-code optional, typed SDKs from OpenAPI, webhooks, a sandbox with a clock.
- **Performance by design.** Immutable, pinned config compiled and cached in-process; rating and decisions evaluated without network hops; stateless horizontal scale; Postgres-only infrastructure to start (pg-boss, no Kafka needed until it is).
- **Source-available core + hosted.** Anyone can read the code and self-host. Revenue from:
  1. **Hosted cloud** (multi-tenant SaaS, SOC 2, backups, scaling).
  2. **Maintained regulatory content**: jurisdiction packs (surplus-lines tax/fee tables, filing deadlines, notice rules, mandatory notice wording) and line-of-business starter products, kept current. This is the moat: rules change constantly, and keeping them right is the job people will pay for.
  3. **Certified adapters** (carriers, stamping offices, data vendors, billing) and enterprise features (SSO/SCIM, advanced audit, data residency, dedicated databases).
- **License: FSL-1.1-ALv2** (Functional Source License 1.1, Apache 2.0 future license; see [`LICENSE`](../LICENSE)). Source-available, not OSI open source: anyone can use, modify and self-host PAS for their own business, but offering it as a competing product or hosted service needs a commercial license. Each release converts to Apache-2.0 two years after it ships. SDKs, the CLI and the config bundle format are MIT so the ecosystem can build on them freely. Watch dependency licenses: no GPL in the core. The spreadsheet rating engine is our own, built on MIT parts (ExcelJS, `fast-formula-parser`, `@formulajs/formulajs`) rather than HyperFormula (GPLv3/commercial).
